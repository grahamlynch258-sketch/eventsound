The honest answer to the LED wall vs projector question is that your room decides most of it. In a space you can properly darken, with a modest audience and a tight budget, a projector remains the sensible choice. And this guide will say so more than once. In a lit room, at scale, in daylight or on camera, an [LED video wall](/services/led-video-walls/) wins on every axis except the smallest invoices. What follows is the comparison in plain terms: how each technology behaves, a side-by-side table, when the projector genuinely is the right call, and what the choice looks like in a typical Irish conference venue.

## The short answer

Choose a **projector** when all three of these are true: the room can be properly darkened, the audience is small enough to sit close to a modest screen, and the budget wants the cheapest workable image.

Choose an **LED wall** when any of these apply: the room stays lit, daylight is involved, the event is filmed or streamed, the audience is large, or the screen is carrying branding and production content rather than just slides.

That's the decision in miniature. The rest of this guide is the reasoning.

## Emitted light versus borrowed light

A projector bounces light off a screen, which means every other light in the room lands on that same screen and washes the image out. Contrast dies first. Blacks turn grey, colours flatten, the picture fades into the wall behind it. This is why cinemas are dark.

An LED wall emits its own light. Every pixel is a tiny lamp. House lights up, daylight through the windows, camera lighting on the presenters: the image holds. Indoor walls run far brighter than projection needs its room to be, and outdoor panels brighter again, because they compete with the sky.

Everything in the comparison below flows from that one difference.

## LED wall vs projector: side by side

| | Projector and screen | LED video wall |
|---|---|---|
| How the image is made | Reflected off a screen | Emitted by the panels |
| Room light | Needs real darkness to look its best | Holds up under full house light and daylight |
| Scale | Practical at typical ballroom screen sizes | Modular, built to the size and shape the room needs |
| Space | Needs throw distance, or depth behind the screen for rear projection | Stands against the stage wall on ground support, or flies from rigging |
| Setup | Quick where the venue is already rigged | Panel-by-panel build. Allow a proper window |
| On camera | Prone to washing out and flickering in shots | Reads cleanly for filming and streaming |
| Mid-show risk | Lamp, alignment and focus issues | Modular. A faulty panel can be swapped |
| Cost shape | Cheapest way to serve a small, dark room | Scales with area. Better value as size and brightness demands grow |
| Best for | Dark rooms, tight budgets, smaller audiences | Lit rooms, large audiences, cameras, outdoor |

## When a projector is the right choice

Candour, as promised. A projector is the right call when:

- **The room genuinely goes dark.** Purpose-built auditoriums and lecture theatres with proper blackout give projection everything it needs. If your venue can kill the light, the biggest argument for LED disappears.
- **The budget is tight and the content is slides.** For a modest room, a screen and a decent projector cost less than any wall we'd build. And a readable deck is a readable deck. Spending the savings on better sound is usually the smarter production decision.
- **The audience is small and close.** Forty people in a seminar room don't need square metres of LED. They need a clear image at a sensible size.
- **The venue already owns decent kit.** Many conference venues have an installed projector and screen. If it's bright enough for the room as you'll actually light it, use it. We'd rather tell you that than hire you something you don't need.

One caveat about the dark room: darkness suits the screen, not the people. Delegates take notes, panellists need their faces lit, dinners need light on the tables. If your format needs the lights on, the projector's ideal conditions are already gone.

## When the LED wall earns its money

- **The lights stay up.** Conference days run lit for note-taking and energy. Awards dinners need the room warm and visible. An LED wall keeps a punchy image in exactly the conditions that ruin projection. At the Mayors Awards in Dublin, our LED wall carried the category content while designed lighting states kept the room alive around it.
- **Daylight is involved.** Atriums, glazed ballrooms, daytime launches and anything outdoor.
- **Cameras are involved.** Filmed and streamed events need presenters lit, and the wall holds its image in shot instead of forcing a choice between readable slides and readable faces.
- **The room is big.** Once a screen must be large to stay legible from the back, projection brightness struggles while LED simply adds panels. Size becomes a build question rather than a brightness one.
- **The screen is the set.** Branding, sponsor loops and full-stage backdrops ask more of a screen than a slide deck does. Modular sizing and custom shapes are the point of LED.

## The hotel ballroom test

Most Irish conference days happen in hotel function rooms, and they're the honest test case. A ballroom rarely offers true blackout: there are windows, the chandeliers stay partly up, and the organiser wants delegates alert rather than dozing in the dark. Projection in those conditions is a compromise from the first slide.

An evening event in the same room shifts the calculation. If dinner is followed by a ten-minute presentation and the lights can drop for it, the installed projector may be perfectly adequate. Say so and save the money. If the screen carries the night, the wall earns its keep: the Music Awards at the Hillgrove Hotel in Monaghan is exactly that format, and it ran on an LED screen we supplied.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a projector always cheaper?**
At seminar-room scale, usually. As screens get bigger and rooms brighter, high-output projection plus screen, rigging and crew narrows the gap, and LED's per-square-metre pricing starts to compare well.

**Can we just dim the lights for the projector?**
You can, and for a short presentation it works. Across a full conference day it fights the format: note-taking, cameras and audience energy all want the lights up.

**What about a large TV instead of either?**
For boardrooms and breakout spaces, individual screens from 43" to 98" are often the right answer. Simpler than projection, cheaper than a wall. Ask us which suits the room.

**Which is better for streaming and filming?**
LED, comfortably. The wall holds exposure alongside lit presenters, while projection forces the camera to choose between a readable screen and readable faces.
