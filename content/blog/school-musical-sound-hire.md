Sound is the part of a school musical or panto that parents notice when it goes wrong and never notice when it goes right. Getting it right means school musical sound hire that covers four things: headset radio mics on the principals, coverage for everyone else, a PA the hall cannot provide, and an operator who mixes every performance. ## What a school musical actually needs from sound

Strip away the kit list and a musical sets four audio jobs. Dialogue must be intelligible at the back row. Solo vocals must sit above a live band. Chorus numbers need lift without a mic on every child. And the cast needs to hear the band clearly enough to stay in time and in tune.

A hall's ceiling speakers were designed for announcements, not for that. We scale a proper PA to the room — line array for bigger, deeper venues and point source for smaller halls — with monitors on stage for foldback.

### Headset mics for the principals

Principal roles go on body-worn headset or lavalier radio mics, worn at the cheek or hairline and taped for choreography. Headsets beat handhelds for a musical because hands are busy and costumes change fast. Our wireless stock is Shure and Sennheiser, which matters less for the badge and more for how they behave in a crowded radio environment with thirty packs running.

### Covering the chorus without forty packs

Nobody needs a transmitter on every member of a fifty-strong chorus, and no budget survives it. Big ensemble numbers are lifted with a small number of area microphones across the front of the stage, mixed in only when the chorus sings. It is not as precise as a personal mic, and it is not meant to be — it reinforces a sound the chorus is already making together.

### The band, playback and foldback

The band eats channels quietly: DIs on keys and bass, mics on drums, brass and reeds, plus a line for backing tracks or click where the show uses them. Foldback matters just as much — young casts drift flat when they cannot hear the band, so stage monitors are part of the spec, not a luxury. If the band will not fit in front of the stage, a low platform sorts sightlines and cabling; see our [stage hire guide](/blog/stage-hire-ireland-guide/) for options.

## How 30 channels disappear: a worked example

Here is a realistic channel plan for a cast-of-forty school musical or a society panto.

| Source | Typical channels |
|---|---|
| Principal cast on headset mics | 12–16 |
| Swap packs for doubled roles and covers | 2–4 |
| Handhelds: MC, panto audience numbers, walk-ons | 2 |
| Area coverage for chorus scenes | 3–4 |
| Band: keys, bass, guitar, drums, brass, reeds | 6–10 |
| Playback and click | 1–2 |
| **Total** | **26–38** |

That total is why our wireless rigs support 30-plus simultaneous channels. It is also why a swap chart matters: when one actor plays three roles, the pack follows a written plan through the wings, not a whispered handover in the dark.

## The operator matters more than the mic count

Theatre mixing is not "set it and forget it". Somebody rides fifteen open vocal channels cue by cue, mutes packs the moment actors exit, catches the missed pickup, and keeps the band under the dialogue. An unmuted mic in a toilet or a green room has ruined more school shows than any equipment failure.

Our quotes are all-inclusive — equipment, delivery, installation, on-site operation and breakdown — which for a show run means the engineer who plots your technical rehearsal is the one mixing every performance. The tech rehearsal is where mic swaps, scene-by-scene levels and problem moments get solved, so protect that date in your schedule.

## Panto season timing: book before the rush

Irish school musicals cluster into late November and December, with a second wave in spring, and panto runs from late November into January. Every group needs the same weekends, and rigs of this size are not infinite. Rehearsals typically start in September; the right moment to book sound is the week your performance dates are confirmed with the venue.

If your show opens in November, the time to lock in kit and crew is now — not at half-term. Late bookings tend to compromise on dates for the technical rehearsal, which is exactly the day you cannot afford to lose.

## Common mistakes with school show sound

1. **Hiring mics without an operator.** Thirty channels do not mix themselves, and a volunteer at an unfamiliar desk is not a fair ask on show night.
2. **No swap plan for doubled roles.** Packs shared on the fly cause missed entrances and open-mic accidents. Write the swap chart into the prompt book.
3. **Skipping the technical rehearsal.** Levels, swaps and band balance get solved at tech. Without it, the first performance becomes the rehearsal.
4. **No foldback.** If the cast cannot hear the band, pitch drifts and tempos wander — and no amount of front-of-house mixing fixes it.
5. **Relying on the hall's ceiling speakers.** They were specified for speech at assembly volume, not for vocals over a live band.
6. **Ignoring batteries and taping.** Fresh batteries each performance and properly taped headsets are boring, cheap and the difference between a clean run and a crackly one.

## Booking checklist

- Cast list with principals, doubled roles and covers marked
- Band line-up, including anything on backing track or click
- Venue name, hall dimensions and what house kit is installed
- Performance schedule, including the technical and dress rehearsals
- Front-of-house mix position — where the desk can physically go
- Who supplies consumables: batteries, mic tape, spare elements
- Budget range, so the channel count can be scaled honestly

## Frequently asked questions

**How many radio mics do we need for a cast of forty?**
Usually 12–16 headsets on principals plus area coverage for the chorus, not forty packs. A swap chart stretches those headsets across doubled roles.

**Do you stay and mix every performance?**
Yes. On-site operation is part of the all-inclusive quote, and the engineer who plots your tech rehearsal runs the shows.

**Can you work with our hall's existing sound system?**
Yes — we integrate with house rigs where they help. For a full musical, the house system usually needs reinforcing with a proper PA and monitors.

**When should we book for panto season?**
As soon as your dates are confirmed. November-to-January weekends are shared by every school and society in the county, and kit books out.
